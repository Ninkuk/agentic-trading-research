## 2. Business

Unchanged from `BR-2026-10-08.md` §2, restated briefly.

**Created:** solves the many-to-many problem between ~1,000 broker-dealers and
effectively every US issuer and fund. Proxy distribution, voting and regulatory
disclosure reach beneficial owners at a mutualized cost no single broker could
match. GTO adds trading, post-trade and wealth platforms that let banks retire
legacy stacks.

**Captured:** four distinct mechanisms:
- (i) per-position regulatory processing fees under the NYSE fee schedule (the
  issuer pays, the broker picks the vendor, the SEC reviews the schedule).
  Q1 regulatory recurring revenue was $230.3M;
- (ii) recurring data, issuer and customer-communication fees ($337.3M in Q1);
- (iii) GTO SaaS and term licenses ($515.5M in Q1);
- (iv) event-driven work ($67.4M in Q1; management expects $60–70M a quarter
  for the rest of FY27).

Distribution ($518.2M in Q1) is low-margin pass-through, mostly postage, and
it is the leg the SEC is modernizing.

**Protected:** SEC and NYSE rules compel broker distribution at a scheduled
fee, so a rival cannot undercut a regulated price, and taking share means
rebuilding ~1,000 broker integrations for a customer who does not pay the
bill. The SEC's comment file SR-NYSE-2020-96 describes BR as handling "almost
all" US beneficial-owner proxy processing. The weakness has the same source:
the rulebook that grants the toll can amend it.

**Control:** one class of common stock, one vote per share, no controlling
holder. Insiders 0.39%, institutions 98.14% [`stocks.db`]. Nothing structural
blocks a takeover or an activist. The annual meeting is 2026-11-10.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive.** The long-run direction holds, and
the trailing year slipped on one quarter's comparison.
