PayPal is a scaled, cash-generative two-sided checkout and wallet network.
Its highest-margin product, branded checkout, grows 1–2% a year while
Venmo, BNPL and Braintree carry the transaction-margin growth. The Q3 print
was the best since the reset: TM$ ex-interest +3%, non-GAAP operating
income +3% (the first rise this year), adjusted FCF guidance raised from at
least $6.0B to at least $6.5B, and more than $700M of run-rate savings
exiting 2026. The stock has re-rated +14% since 10-03, and the margin has
shrunk with it. At 9.4× SBC-adjusted trailing FCF the price now discounts
flat owner cash flow, not the −3%/yr decline it discounted in October. The
central row clears the hurdle by ~90–124bp; the cash-tax-normalised row
sits at the hurdle. This is still "a decent business at a fair-to-slightly-
cheap price, with a 12% buyback yield." It is no longer "priced for
decline." Above $66 the central row stops clearing, and that is a pass.

## 2. Business

In substance unchanged from `research/PYPL-2026-10-03.md` §2. The Q3 deltas:

**Created:** 440M active accounts (+0.6%), 6.8B payment transactions (+7%),
TPV $487.1B in Q3 (+7% FXN). Consumers get a stored wallet with buyer
protection, BNPL and one-touch checkout. Merchants get the consumers,
conversion, risk absorption and one integration across ~200 markets. Venmo
TPV has grown double digits for eight straight quarters. Venmo debit has
6.7M monthly actives (+55%) and Pay with Venmo grew +43%. (Q3 8-K Ex-99.1;
call via MarketBeat.)

**Captured:** the same seven mechanisms as before:

1. Branded checkout fees.
2. Braintree/PSP processing at thin margin. The transaction expense rate
   was 0.87%, from 0.89%.
3. Credit: BNPL volume +25%, PayPal Credit and merchant lending.
   "Financial services" is now >20% of TM$ and growing double digits.
4. Interest on customer balances: $289M in Q3, down from $321M.
5. Venmo monetisation.
6. FX and instant-transfer fees.
7. OVAS partnerships.

Transaction margin was 46.0%, flat YoY. FY26 TM$ is guided to ~$15.7B,
raised from ~$15.6B.

**Protected:** a two-sided habit — stored funding instruments and the button
on most e-commerce checkouts — that holds the base, not the growth. Branded
+1% against TPV +7% is share loss at the margin. What still defends it:
Venmo's US P2P graph, a risk/credit stack running a 0.08% loss rate (from
0.11%), the merchant integration footprint, and the fact that no rival
wallet is two-sided across ~200 markets. The moat is a mechanism, not a
checkbox, but it is narrowing at the edge.

**Control:** one class of common stock, 830.7M shares (10-Q cover,
2026-10-21), no controlling holder. Insiders hold 0.11% and institutions
