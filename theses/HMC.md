## 0. The reopen question, answered first

The print was strong underneath and ugly on the headline. H1 EV losses were
¥484.8B, so the literal ≤ ¥260B test **fired**. The full-year figure did not
move (¥520B), and the 6-K note shows why the losses came early: ¥508.7B of
supplier compensation for cancelled EV parts, the claim that was unmeasurable
in the 20-F and the Q1 6-K, is now provisioned "based on discussions with the
suppliers". The ¥260B threshold assumed the ¥520B would land pro rata, and a
lump-sum provision never does. So the miss is timing, not a breach.
Cumulative EV losses are ~¥2.1T (FY26 ¥1,577.8B plus the FY27 ¥520B) against
the ¥2.5T envelope, and the largest unmeasured item is now measured.
**Condition 1 moves from plausible to probable.** That is a judgement call;
the prior flip-evidence sentence was mis-specified, not the thesis.

The FY27 operating guide went from ¥650B to ¥780B (adjusted ¥1.17T → ¥1.30T).
No Canada plan was stated: management called Canada-to-US tariffs "a new risk
factor" with no amount included, and kept a ¥140B H2 tariff allowance partly
because of it. The price is $34.30, nowhere near $27. **The call stays BUY,
with a thinner cushion.** The EV risk got smaller. Two new facts make the
cushion thinner: FY27 capex jumps to ¥1.4T (from ¥751B) for a new Ohio hybrid
plant and a bigger Manaus motorcycle plant, and management named ~¥1.1T, not
~¥1.2T, as the FY28 starting point.

3.3× depreciation. The cash the bull case was counting on is going into
plants that pay back from 2029, so the margin of safety now rests on that
capex earning something.
