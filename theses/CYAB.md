## 2. Business

**Created:** Cyabra's software "identifies bad actors online, and mitigates
the threat in real-time" (10-Q 2026-08-13). It flags inauthentic profiles,
coordinated networks and disinformation narratives aimed at a brand or a
government. Brands buy it for early warning on reputational attacks. The
shareholder letter (2026-05-05) names PepsiCo, e.l.f. Beauty and IPG.
Governments buy it for influence-operation detection; the letter names NATO
and Korea's MFA, and an 8-K/press release names an APAC intelligence agency.
The customer surplus is speed: seeing a fake-account campaign hours before it
trends.

**Captured:** three mechanisms (10-Q revenue-recognition note):
(a) **SaaS subscriptions**: hosted access, non-refundable, terms from several
months to three years, billed in advance and recognised ratably. This is the
main line.
(b) **On-premises licences** for government agencies hosting it themselves:
two- to three-year terms, recognised ratably.
(c) **Managed-service reports**, one-off analyst-prepared packages, often a
paid trial before a subscription, recognised on delivery.
Deal size is "six-figure" per announced win (Fortune 500 consumer brand;
two-year management-agency renewal, 2026-04-14). Gross margin is 85% TTM
($5.431M / $6.384M, stockanalysis). ARR is defined in the 10-Q, but this run
did not obtain the figure (§6).

**Protected:** no mechanism disclosed. The S-1 (2026-10-08) names Logically,
Blackbird.AI, Alethea Group, VineSight and Pyrra as competitors, all private
and similarly sized. Nothing in the filings describes proprietary data access,
switching costs beyond contract term, or a network effect. The product's input
is public social-media data, which large platforms and threat-intel vendors
can also read. The silence is the answer: **no moat evidenced**.

**Control:** one class of common stock, one vote per share, no cumulative
voting, quorum one-third (DEF 14A 2026-10-15). No controlling holder. The
largest holders are Trailblazer Sponsor Group 12.27%, Alpha Capital 9.99% and
Armistice 9.99% (both behind 9.99% blockers on far larger warrant positions),
RainForest 6.90% and Hewlett Fund 6.82%. D&Os together own 10.87%. The board
is classified (three classes), which slows an activist. The real control
lever is contractual: the PIPE holders' warrant stack sets the supply of
stock.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): negative.** Revenue rose ~8× while the
operating loss more than tripled. Opex grew faster than gross profit in every
year. The TTM figure includes ~$3.4M of non-recurring merger costs and
front-loaded SBC in Q1'26. Ex those, the Q2'26 run-rate (−$3.12M operating,
−$2.88M adj. EBITDA) is flat to slightly worse y/y. It is not improving.
