## 2. Business

**Created:** printers, materials and contract parts for applications where a
printed part beats a cast or machined one: dental aligner and denture materials,
orthopedic implants in titanium and PEEK, rocket and naval components that avoid
12–24 month casting lead times (Q3 call). The customer gets speed, geometry and
qualified process, not a low unit price.

**Captured:** four mechanisms. Printer sales (lumpy, tied to capex and
procurement cycles). Consumable materials, the recurring pull-through on the
installed base, where one dental customer's volumes swing results. Services and
maintenance, $163.5M of 2025's $386.9M. Parts manufacturing, a newer contract
line with 77 metal and 42 polymer printers in production and a new 50,000 sq ft
Littleton site, which is more capital-intensive than selling a machine.
Printer/materials revenue is not separately printed (§6).

**Protected:** a partial mechanism, not a moat. Regulated and qualified
applications (dental and implant workflows, aerospace and naval parts) make a
validated material and process costly to switch; that is my read, not a filing's.
It protects the installed application, not the market: the 10-K names no
competitors, but Stratasys and others sell into the same verticals, and a 34.5%
trailing gross margin is not what pricing power looks like. Patents fell to 854 from 1,247 in 2025
(10-K); the part of that due to the software sales is UNKNOWN.

**Control:** one class of common, one vote per share, no controlling holder.
State Street 7.78%, BlackRock 6.56%, Bank of Nova Scotia 5.19%; directors and
officers 3.99% (proxy, 2026-03-23). Annual board, majority voting. No poison pill
appears in the proxy pages read. Nothing structural forecloses an activist or an
unsolicited bid.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): negative.** Over the full record revenue fell 36%
while the operating loss doubled. The last seven quarters run the other way, but
that is cost removal, not volume: TTM revenue is up 2.3% on 2025.
