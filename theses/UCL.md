## 2. Business

**Created:** a traveller gets working mobile data abroad without buying a local
SIM or paying carrier roaming. uCloudlink pools SIMs from 398 network operators
and assigns one over the air to a hotspot, phone or IoT device. The surplus is real for groups sharing a hotspot and for phones without
eSIM support. It is thin for a solo traveller with an eSIM phone.

**Captured:** four mechanisms, Q2 2026 of $18.2M revenue.

- Data connectivity, $10.2M: international ("1.0") $8.2M, down from $10.0M, and
  local ("2.0") $2.0M, up from $1.5M. Services margin is 59.1%.
- Product sales, $4.9M, at a 26.5% margin, down from 41.0% on memory cost.
- PaaS/SaaS, $2.5M, down 6.3%, sold to partners.
- By line: MeowGo $15.4M (−13.1%), SIM $1.3M (+78%), IoT $0.8M (+392%), Life
  $0.5M (−21%), PetPhone $0.2M.

Geography: Japan 36.0%, mainland China 30.3%, North America 13.5%, other 20.2%.
Only 12,763 of 341,511 daily terminals are company-owned, so the model is
genuinely capital-light on terminals.

**Protected:** a mechanism, but a narrow one. 184 approved patents and the
398-operator SIM pool make the cloud-SIM hotspot hard to copy, and I expect
operator relationships take years to rebuild. That protects the hotspot, not the need for one. eSIM substitutes
for the hotspot for most solo travellers at about $18–25 against $40–70 for a
week in Japan (vendor marketing, low-confidence). MeowGo DAU falling 7.3% while
total DAU rises 13.3% fits that. The 20-F's competition section names no rival.
I read it as a moat around a shrinking use case.

**Control:** dual-class, 15 votes per Class B share. Chaohui Chen (MediaPlay
Limited, 61,346,560 Class B) and Zhiping Peng (AlphaGo Robot Limited,
60,726,420 Class B) hold all 122,072,980 Class B, 32.0% of share capital and
87.6% of the vote at 2026-02-28. On Class B alone that is about 44.0% and 43.6%
(derived), so neither controls alone and together they decide everything. An
unsolicited takeover and an activist path are both foreclosed. Foreign private
issuer following some Cayman home-country practices. Institutions hold 0.27%.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): negative.** Revenue is down 8% from 2023 and
operating income has swung by $9.6M.

| 2021 | $73.8M | −$32.5M | −$46.0M | −$22.5M |
| 2022 | $71.5M | −$3.9M | −$19.9M | $4.0M |

The stockanalysis operating line differs from the company's, which folds in

profit less opex of $1.3M. A $4.6M fair-value gain on the fund sits in that
gap, so 2025's $6.3M net income overstates the business; adjusted net income
was $2.7M.
