## 2. Business

Unchanged from `research/EEFT-2026-09-07.md` §2. In brief:

**Created:** physical and licensed reach a customer cannot build alone:
ATMs and currency conversion where a traveller's bank has no presence,
prepaid content at cash-paying corner shops, and payout points in the
recipient's town for migrant senders.

**Captured:** per-transaction fees through six mechanisms: ATM withdrawal
fees and DCC spread, merchant discount on acquiring, SaaS processing fees on
Ren/CoreCard, epay distribution commissions, remittance send fee plus FX
spread, and Dandelion wholesale payout fees. Q3 2026 [8-K ex-99.1]: PI
$437.9M revenue (+7%) / $128.2M op income (+0.1%); epay $294.7M (+3%) /
$31.8M (+3%); Cross-Border $431.0M (−5%) / $37.3M (−37%). Cross-Border is
now 18.9% of segment operating income ($37.3M of $197.3M).

**Protected:** density and per-country licensing. These are slow, capital-
and regulator-heavy to replicate, but they protect the *position*, not the
*price*, and do nothing against contactless adoption or immigration
enforcement. A shallow moat.

**Control:** one class of common, one share one vote, no controlling
holder; insiders 7.85% (`stocks.db`). Nothing forecloses an unsolicited
approach or an activist campaign.

**Delta:** three facts are new since the prior run. First, about half of
CoreCard's revenue is going away as the Apple card program moves to
JPMorgan Chase, with a goal to replace "90%–100%" of it (CEO, Q3 call).
Second, Steam stopped selling physical gift cards at the end of October, a
Q4 epay headwind (Q3 call). Third, Ria pricing is down ~2–3% YoY (~1–1.5%
on gross profit), and ~$6M of incremental advertising sat in Cross-Border's
Q3 (Q3 call).

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): negative in 2026** (positive FY2021→2025).
