made a full-year operating profit, whose operating leverage turned negative in

so the line returns only after reformulation, on no stated date, at no

about the cost of equity; there is no margin of safety for a brand whose
whole proposition is parental trust, nine days before a print that will

unknowable — four of five simply await dated disclosures, and the price

## 2. Business

**Created:** refrigerated (cold-pressed / HPP) organic fruit-and-veg pouches,
frozen meals, and soft-baked bars for babies, toddlers and kids, sold from
retail coolers, club, e-commerce and DTC (stockanalysis profile; Q2 2026
call). The parent buys trust — clean-label, organic, refrigerated rather than
shelf-stable — and convenience. Management's own evidence that the surplus is
real: repeat among households with kids 52.1%, protein pouches 61–63%
incremental to brand and category at some retailers (Q2 call).

**Captured:** three distinct mechanisms. (1) A price premium over shelf-stable
pouches, protected only by the refrigerated format and brand; gross margin
35.9–47.7% across the last four quarters versus a 23.2% food-processing
median (Damodaran, Jan 2026). (2) Cooler placement — ~4,000 retail doors
today, targeting ~5,000 in 2026, 8,000 in 2027, 15,000+ eventually, at
$8k–$50k per cooler per year (Q2 call): shelf space the company effectively
owns inside a retailer. (3) Club and national programs (the Q2 club program;
a second national Tractor Wheels program planned for Q3) that buy trial with
trade spend — which is the main reason gross margin fell 485bp YoY in Q2.

**Protected:** weakly. The refrigerated cold chain and cooler slots are a
real but copyable position — a large incumbent (Gerber, Danone's Happy Baby)
or private label can fund coolers too, and management names private label as
its main competition. The brand is the moat, and the brand is precisely what
a choking recall on a toddler product attacks. Production runs through "a
limited number of independent contract manufacturers… including co-packers"
(10-Q risk language), so there is no manufacturing moat; automation capex of
$25–35M is planned to change that (Q2 call). Honest answer: a trust-and-shelf
position, not a structural moat.

**Control:** one class of common stock (41,990,653 shares at 2026-07-31; 10-Q
cover), NYSE. Delaware **public benefit corporation** — directors must balance
stakeholder interests against shareholder value, which gives the board cover
to refuse a purely price-driven bid. Insiders 13.97%, institutions 45.27%
(stockanalysis). No controlling holder identified; 13G filers' identities not
obtained. Float is reported at 15.66M (stockanalysis) vs 20.19M (Robinhood).

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): negative** (latest year; positive FY2023–25).

|---|---|---|---|
| FY2023 | $94.3M | −$15.3M | −16.2% |

Revenue grew 3.06× while the operating loss went from −$15.3M to −$5.7M and
back out to −$18.3M. Ex-SBC it is still negative: TTM ≈ −$9.5M (SBC $8.83M)
vs FY2025 ≈ −$1.8M (SBC $3.92M). The last-twelve-month turn comes from gross
margin compression (trade spend, snack mix, fuel, tariffs) and IPO-related
SBC and performance payments (~$3.5M inside Q2 SG&A). Quarterly operating
income: −9.19, +0.02, −1.05, +4.52, −16.15, −5.64 ($M, Q1'25→Q2'26).
Persistently loss-making — Phase 0's own kill flag; run continued as a
judgment call (unattended) because net cash removes the leverage leg of the
kill and the recall dislocation is the question worth answering.
