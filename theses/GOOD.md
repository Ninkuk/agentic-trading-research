## 2. Business

**Created:** Mid-size companies get a single-tenant industrial or office
building, often through a sale-leaseback, without tying up their own capital
in real estate. 110 tenants, 21 industries, 27 states, 7.1-year average
remaining term (Q3 supplement). Tenants come for capital and speed; a lessor
of this size will underwrite private credits. 61% of rent comes from private
companies and 46% from tenants that are non-investment-grade or the
equivalent, which is why the yields are high.

**Captured:** Three mechanisms. Contract rent on net leases, $143.6M
annualized straight-line, with reimbursement of most property costs.
Contractual escalators of about 2% or more (management, Q3 release). Capital
recycling: buying at cap rates near 9% with cheap fixed or swapped debt, which
only earns a spread while debt stays cheaper than the entry yield.

**Protected:** No moat. Any capital provider can bid on the same buildings.
The only protection is the lease term and the tenant's cost of moving, which
last as long as the lease. Management says competition is tightening and
credit quality is worsening in some deals (Q3 call).

**Control:** One common class at one vote each, but no realistic outside
path. David Gladstone, chairman, owns and controls the external Adviser and
Administrator, which employ all staff. The charter caps any holder at 9.8%,
the board is staggered with removal only for cause, and the Maryland business
combination statute applies. Ending the advisory agreement without cause takes
120 days' notice, two-thirds of independent directors and a fee of twice the
average annual fees. Activism and an unsolicited bid are effectively
foreclosed.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive at the operating line, negative per
share.** Operating income grew faster than revenue, but interest and share
count consumed it:
