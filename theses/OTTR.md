## 2. Business

**Created:** Three businesses under one ticker. (a) Otter Tail Power
delivers electricity across a rural service territory in western Minnesota,
eastern North Dakota and northeastern South Dakota — a franchise monopoly;
the customer gets reliable power at a regulated price and has no
alternative wire. (b) Plastics (Northern Pipe Products, ND; Vinyltech, AZ)
extrudes PVC pipe sold mainly in the western US and Canada (FY2025 10-K) — a
commodity converter whose value to a distributor is regional freight
advantage and availability, not product differentiation. (c) Manufacturing
(contract metal fabrication and thermoforming) — job-shop work for RV,
lawn-and-garden, ag, construction and industrial OEMs (Q3 call).

**Captured:** Three distinct mechanisms.
- *Electric:* cost-of-service regulation — an allowed return on rate base
  (allowed ROE MN 9.48%, ND 10.10%, SD 8.75%; allowed return on rate base
  7.18%/7.53%/7.09%, FY2025 10-K) plus pass-through riders for fuel and
  transmission. Earnings grow only as fast as approved rate base. Two
  customers are 16% of Electric revenue (FY2025 10-K).
- *Plastics:* the spread between PVC pipe selling price and PVC resin cost
  (four resin vendors supplied all 2025 needs, 10-K Item 1A). That spread
  exploded in 2021–2022 and is still compressing from both sides: Q3 2026
  price −9% YoY, volume −5%, resin and inputs +10% (Q3 release).
- *Manufacturing:* job-shop margins; steel costs passed through (Q3
  release).

**Protected:** Electric — the exclusive service-territory franchise is a
legal moat, and what it protects is a *fair* return, not an excess one
(allowed ROEs of 8.75–10.10% sit near a utility cost of equity). Plastics —
no moat: a commodity product with 20+ named co-defendants in the
price-fixing suits, regional freight the only edge, and the 2021–2025
margins have proved transient. Manufacturing — no moat beyond customer
relationships.

**Control:** One class of common shares, $5 par, Nasdaq (FY2025 10-K cover);
41.99M shares out; insiders 2.0%, institutions ~78% (stockanalysis). No
controlling holder; nothing forecloses a takeover or an activist path. The
real takeover friction is that a utility sale needs MN/ND/SD commission
approval.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): negative** since the 2022 peak. It was
positive over the full 2020→TTM span, but only because the Plastics
windfall entered.
