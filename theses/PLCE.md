## 2. Business

**Created:** value-priced apparel for infants and children. The 10-K describes
the customer as a "value-conscious, lower- to middle-income mother" buying on
need more than fashion. The surplus is price and convenience, which Carter's,
Target, Old Navy, GapKids, T.J. Maxx, Burlington, Kohl's, and Walmart all offer
too (10-K Item 1A competitor list).

**Captured:** three mechanisms. (1) 514 owned stores at 08-01 across the US,
Canada, and Puerto Rico, on product margin. (2) Direct e-commerce, down 15.0% in
Q2 and mid-migration to Salesforce, which management says "has also taken longer
than anticipated". (3) Wholesale and franchise through ten partners in 13
countries, with planned wholesale reductions in Q2. Q2 net sales by the 10-Q
geography note: South $96.5M, Northeast $41.2M, West $29.0M, Midwest $21.0M,
International and other $54.1M. The Children's Place and Gymboree are the two
primary brands.

**Protected:** no moat. Nothing stops a competitor tomorrow, and the competitors
are bigger and already there. The Gymboree tradename carries at $13.0M. The
Children's Place name is unbooked, and it is the one real asset, which is why
the reported bids are for IP plus about 200 stores rather than for the company.

**Control:** one class of common. Mithaq Capital SPC and related persons
beneficially own 13,593,236 shares, 61.1% (13D/A No. 10, event 2026-08-11;
Alrajhi and Seemab are reporting persons), so PLCE is a Nasdaq controlled
company and DGCL 203 limits are a stated risk factor. Mithaq is also the largest
subordinated creditor at $151.1M principal. An unsolicited bid or activist path
is foreclosed; the remaining decision-makers are the senior lenders and, after
any filing, the court.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): negative.** Revenue fell 41% from FY ending
2022-01 to TTM while operating income fell $359.7M, a 45.5% decremental margin
(50.5% with the refund stripped).
