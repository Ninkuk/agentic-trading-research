## 2. Business

Unchanged from `GMED-2026-10-03.md` §2 (itself carried from
`GMED-2026-09-02.md`); restated in brief.

**Created:** a surgeon gets a complete consigned kit — implants,
instruments, biologics, navigation — and a rep who knows the technique,
backed by a fast launch cadence. The surplus is time and confidence at the
table, not price.

**Captured:** (i) per-procedure implant and disposable sales
(Musculoskeletal Solutions, $2,797.9M FY25); (ii) ExcelsiusGPS/Hub capital
sales, leases and rentals (Enabling Technologies, $141.0M FY25); (iii)
services — neuromonitoring and robot service; (iv) Nevro spinal-cord-
stimulation devices, ~$325M annualised. International 19.4% of FY25 sales.

**Protected:** the exclusive rep in the OR with a consigned set, plus a
robot on the floor whose trained users and stored screw plans make an
implant switch costly for the surgeon; in-house manufacturing funds rep
density. Symmetric weakness: reps are poachable — ATEC's +25% net new
surgeon users is that weakness running the other way.

**Control:** 111.8M Class A (one vote) and 22.4M Class B (ten votes);
founder David C. Paul holds 99.2% of Class B for 66.2% of the vote; NYSE
controlled company, classified board (DEF 14A, 2026-04-24). Forecloses an
unsolicited takeover and any activist path; succession is a single-person
risk.
