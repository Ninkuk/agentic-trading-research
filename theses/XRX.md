## 2. Business

**Created:** Xerox (with Lexmark, acquired July 1, 2025) places office and
production printers and multifunction devices, then services them for years:
toner, parts, managed-print contracts, and a smaller IT-services reselling
arm. The customer gets uptime and a single throat to choke for a fleet of
devices; the surplus is convenience and contract inertia, not price or
technology. The installed base is shrinking with page volumes.

**Captured:** four distinct streams, from the Q3 2026 release:
- *Equipment sale* ($393M in Q3, +3%): one-time hardware margin; mix is
  shifting from mid-range (−15%) to lower-margin entry devices (+21%).
- *Post-sale* ($1.3B in Q3, −5%): service contracts, supplies and
  per-page charges on the installed base — the annuity, and the part that
  is declining.
- *Xerox Financial Services* ($55M financing revenue, −22.5%): lease
  financing for customer purchases, funded by ~$1.3B of financing debt;
  being deliberately shrunk (net finance receivables $3.1B in 2022 → $1.2B)
  and sold (~$300M of receivable sales in 2026).
- *IT Solutions* ($187M, −17%, 4.4% segment margin): resold hardware and
  services; delayed projects and tighter distributor terms.

**Protected:** a weak, decaying moat. The installed base and multi-year
service contracts create switching friction, and the Lexmark merger buys
scale in a consolidating oligopoly (HP, Canon, Ricoh, Konica Minolta,
Kyocera, Brother). Nothing protects against the real competitor — fewer
printed pages. The $959M Q3 goodwill impairment on Print and Other is
management conceding that the long-term forecast is lower.

**Control:** one share, one vote, no controller. The Deason trust holds
11.29% (15.28M shares including 6.74M issuable on its 180,000 Series A
preferred, 13D event 2026-02-06); STARTEEPO SICAV / Frantisek Bostl hold
7.34% (13D/A Amendment No. 3, 2026-09-08) and publicly urge a strategic
review of Xerox Financial Services. The structure forecloses nothing; the
practical constraint is the creditors — the first-lien ad hoc group
(Moelis-advised) and covenants now restricting the IPCo joint venture.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): negative.** Revenue rose only through the
Lexmark acquisition while operating income fell by more than half from 2021:

| year | revenue | operating income | levered FCF |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2021 | $7.038B | $409M | $561M |
| 2022 | $7.107B | $224M | $102M |
| 2023 | $6.886B | $327M | $649M |
| 2024 | $6.221B | $130M | $467M |
| 2025 | $7.022B | $15M | $133M |
| TTM Q3 2026 | $7.664B | $233M | $22M |

(stockanalysis income and cash-flow statements). Revenue +9% over the span
(all acquired), operating income −43%, FCF −96%.
